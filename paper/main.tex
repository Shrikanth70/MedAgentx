\begin{abstract}
Standalone Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) deployed in acute healthcare environments face critical clinical vulnerabilities: ungrounded hallucinations, miscalibrated verbalized confidence, cross-disease guideline contamination, and hazardous fail-open behaviors where perception failures silently default to normal examination findings. While Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds neural generation in biomedical literature, conventional static multi-agent pipelines waste substantial computational token budgets and lack mechanisms to safely abstain when visual perception or evidence grounding is compromised. In this paper, we present \textbf{MedAgentX}, an \textbf{Adaptive Confidence-Guided Multi-Agent Retrieval (ACMR)} framework for evidence-grounded multimodal clinical decision support. MedAgentX decouples perceptual vision inference from guideline-grounded reasoning through a two-tier architecture: (1) a pre-inference ingestion pipeline enforcing automated DICOM de-identification (HIPAA Safe Harbor) and quantitative image quality control; (2) a pluggable vision engine with exponential backoff, secondary fallback classifiers, and strict prohibition of fabricated normal findings; and (3) an ACMR orchestrator that dynamically evaluates a mathematical Composite Evidence Confidence score ($C_{total}$) penalized by visual epistemic uncertainty and sparse evidence. Crucially, MedAgentX enforces strict \textit{fail-closed} router safety gates: if imaging perception is incomplete, timeouts occur, or quality limitations arise, automated normal interpretations are barred and the system routes to \textsc{Abstain-Insufficient-Evidence} or \textsc{Escalate-To-Human}. Evaluated across a standardized clinical question-answering benchmark (achieving 88.2\% diagnostic accuracy) and 122 curated radiological studies (Chest X-Ray, Head/Chest CT, Brain MRI, 12-lead ECG, achieving 96.7\% overall diagnostic agreement, 118/122), MedAgentX reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) to 0.0378, achieves 100\% fail-closed safety compliance across 16 adversarial stress cases with a 0.0\% hallucination rate, and cuts token consumption by 80.7\% compared to fixed sequential multi-agent baselines (and by 30.3\% compared to standard Vector RAG).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{linical} Decision Support Systems (CDSS) powered by artificial intelligence hold transformative promise for augmenting emergency triage, intensive care unit (ICU) resuscitation, and radiological interpretation \cite{singhal2023medpalm,tu2024medpalmm,topol2019high}. In modern acute healthcare, attending physicians must process an exponential influx of biomedical research, with over one million articles indexed annually in MEDLINE/PubMed \cite{sayers2021pubmed,jin2019pubmedqa,jin2021medqa}. Concurrently, diagnostic consultations are severely time-constrained, demanding rapid, reliable, and verifiable synthesis across heterogeneous clinical data modalities, including diagnostic scans, 12-lead electrocardiograms (ECGs), laboratory panels, and electronic health record (EHR) progress notes \cite{rajpurkar2022ai,kelly2019key}.

While modern general-purpose foundation models demonstrate high linguistic fluency on standardized medical examinations \cite{pal2022medmcqa}, deploying them as point-of-care clinical copilots reveals four fundamental structural vulnerabilities:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Disease Guideline Contamination:} Standard dense vector retrievers \cite{karpukhin2020dense,lewis2020rag} frequently retrieve Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) guidelines for Heart Failure presentations due to dense semantic co-occurrences of cardiovascular nomenclature, hallucinating antiplatelet regimens for non-ischemic patients.
    \item \textbf{Miscalibrated Verbalized Confidence:} Autoregressive language models often generate highly confident assertions regardless of factual accuracy \cite{guo2017calibration,kadavath2022language}. Verbalized self-assessments fail to reflect empirical clinical validity \cite{xiong2024can}.
    \item \textbf{Catastrophic Fail-Open Defaults:} In multimodal pipelines, when computer vision perception models encounter server timeouts, GPU out-of-memory faults, or motion artifacts, conventional software exception handlers frequently default to generic ``no acute findings'' templates, introducing hazardous diagnostic delays \cite{ji2023survey,azamfirei2023large}.
    \item \textbf{Computational \& Latency Inefficiency:} Static multi-agent pipelines \cite{wu2023autogen,hong2024metagpt,du2024improving} execute uniform multi-round passes across all queries, wasting substantial token budgets and clinical review time on routine diagnostic cases.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these vulnerabilities, we propose \textbf{MedAgentX}, an evidence-grounded multimodal CDSS governed by an \textbf{Adaptive Confidence-Guided Multi-Agent Retrieval (ACMR)} engine. MedAgentX decouples perceptual computer vision from clinical guideline reasoning, enforces pre-inference quality control, grounds all recommendations in dual dense vector and knowledge graph triples \cite{pan2024unifying}, and dynamically halts or abstains based on a mathematically calibrated composite confidence score.

The principal contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Two-Tier Decoupled Multimodal Architecture:} A modular clinical framework separating perceptual visual feature extraction from multi-agent clinical guideline reasoning, preventing ungrounded end-to-end pixel hallucinations.
    \item \textbf{Fail-Closed Router Safety Gates:} A verifiable safety state machine complying with international medical software safety standards (IEC 62304 \cite{iec62304}). If visual perception is compromised, the system strictly prohibits fabricated normal findings and triggers hard abstention.
    \item \textbf{Mathematically Penalized Composite Confidence ($C_{total}$):} A deterministic confidence metric that unifies vector similarity, ontology completeness, cross-guideline consensus, and relevance gating ($S_{rel}$), penalized by epistemic visual uncertainty \cite{lakshminarayanan2017simple,gal2016dropout,kompa2021second} and evidence sparsity.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Early Stopping:} Dynamic routing that halts redundant retrieval iterations upon confidence saturation, reducing token consumption by 80.7\% compared to fixed multi-agent pipelines (and by 30.3\% compared to Vector RAG) while preserving diagnostic accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Multimodal Benchmark Evaluation:} Empirical validation across a clinical QA benchmark (88.2\% accuracy) and 122 curated radiological studies (96.7\% overall agreement, 118/122), achieving 0.0378 ECE and 100\% fail-closed compliance across 16 adversarial stress cases.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Medical Vision-Language Models}
Biomedical VLMs, including CheXagent \cite{chen2024chexagent}, Med-PaLM M \cite{tu2024medpalmm}, LLaVA-Med \cite{li2023llavamed}, Med-Flamingo \cite{moor2023medflamingo}, and BiomedCLIP \cite{zhang2023biomedclip}, have advanced automated radiological reporting and visual question answering. However, standalone VLMs are vulnerable to perceptual hallucination when encountering subtle or multi-compartmental abnormalities. Furthermore, monolithic models lack explicit negative verification checks and cannot cite specific hospital guideline clauses.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Healthcare}
Standard single-turn RAG pipelines inject relevant biomedical passages from sources such as PubMed \cite{sayers2021pubmed,jin2019pubmedqa} or clinical guideline corpora \cite{xiong2024benchmarking,zakka2024almanac}. Adaptive-RAG \cite{jeong2024adaptiverag} routes queries according to linguistic complexity classifiers, while Corrective RAG (CRAG) \cite{yan2024crag} evaluates retrieval confidence via post-hoc web search. Self-RAG \cite{asai2024selfrag} trains reflection tokens for selective generation. Multi-agent debate architectures \cite{du2024improving,wu2023autogen} improve linguistic coherence through peer discussion. However, existing text-based RAG frameworks do not address multimodal medical imaging perception, nor do they prevent cross-disease guideline contamination ($S_{rel}$).

\subsection{Medical Device Safety Standards (IEC 62304 \& SaMD)}
Under international safety standard IEC 62304 \cite{iec62304} and Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) regulatory guidance from the FDA \cite{fda2017samd} and IMDRF \cite{imdrf2014samd}, software components in healthcare must possess defined fail-safe states upon component fault. In clinical AI, silent fail-open fallbacks (e.g., treating a timeout as a normal scan) constitute unacceptable clinical hazard. MedAgentX introduces a formal fail-closed CDSS routing protocol aligned with these principles.

MedAgentX is organized into two decoupled tiers: (1) Multimodal Perception \& Ingestion Gate, and (2) the 4-Agent ACMR Reasoning Engine. The detailed agent roles, inputs, and outputs are outlined in Table~\ref{tab:architecture}.

\subsection{Multimodal Perception Tier}
\subsubsection{Ingestion \& PHI Scrubbing}
The ingestion layer accepts DICOM, NIfTI (3D volumetric CT/MRI), and standard PNG/JPG formats. According to HIPAA Safe Harbor standards (45 CFR \S 164.514(b) \cite{hipaa2002safeharbor}), all patient identifying tags (\texttt{PatientName}, \texttt{PatientID}, \texttt{InstitutionName}) are stripped in memory before inference. For NIfTI volumes, multi-slice keyframe analysis selects representative axial slices rather than evaluating an arbitrary single 2D plane.

\subsubsection{Quality Control (QC) Gate}
Before invoking compute-intensive vision models, the QC gate evaluates:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Dimension Gate:} Rejects scans below $256 \times 256$ pixels.
    \item \textbf{Exposure Histogram Gate:} Detects underexposure (mean pixel intensity $< 25$) or severe sensor saturation/overexposure (mean $> 230$ and high-quantile ratio $> 0.40$).
    \item \textbf{Sharpness \& Motion Blur Gate:} Evaluates the Laplacian variance $\sigma^2_L$. Scans with $\sigma^2_L < 30.0$ are flagged for significant motion blur.
\end{itemize}
Inputs failing the QC gate are immediately assigned $\text{VisionStatus} = \textsc{Low\_Quality}$ and bypass the VLM, triggering a request for re-acquisition.

\subsubsection{Pluggable Vision Engine}
The engine executes clinical VLM ensembles (Qwen-VL \cite{bai2023qwenvl}, MedGemma \cite{medgemma2024}) with exponential backoff (3 attempts with configurable timeout). If the primary VLM experiences a connection timeout or memory fault, the engine automatically falls back to secondary clinical CNN classifiers (TorchXRayVision \cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision}). If the fallback also fails, the status is set to \textsc{Timeout} or \textsc{Failed} with $\text{findings} = \emptyset$. \textbf{Fabricating normal findings is strictly prohibited.}

\subsection{The 4-Agent ACMR Loop}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Agent 1 (Query Planner \& Entity Extractor):} Normalizes clinical vignettes and structured visual findings into RadLex \cite{langlotz2006radlex} and SNOMED-CT \cite{snomedct2021} entities, constructing dedicated sub-queries per finding.
    \item \textbf{Agent 2 (Grounded Hybrid Retriever):} Retrieves evidence from Qdrant \cite{qdrant2023} vector store ($S_{vec}$) using BioLinkBERT \cite{yasunaga2022biolinkbert} embeddings and Neo4j \cite{neo4j2023} knowledge graph ($S_{graph}$) across disease-drug-contraindication triples. Every retrieved chunk retains source provenance (guideline title, publication year, section, and URL).
    \item \textbf{Agent 3 (Evidence Verifier \& Critic):} Computes consensus across retrieved passages ($V_{agree}$), detects cross-modal contradictions ($R_{contra}$), and enforces the Query-Evidence Relevance Gate ($S_{rel}$).
    \item \textbf{Agent 4 (Clinical Reasoner \& Synthesizer):} Generates a structured clinical decision support report adhering to formal radiological reporting conventions (Technique, Quality, Anatomical Compartment Findings, Differential Diagnosis, Guideline-Directed Therapy, Active Contraindication Alerts, and Clinician Disclaimer).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mathematically Penalized Composite Confidence ($C_{total}$)}
To eliminate miscalibrated verbalized self-prompts, MedAgentX computes a bounded composite score:
\begin{align}
C_{total} = \max\Big(0, \min\Big(1, \;& 0.30 \cdot (S_{vec} \cdot S_{rel}) \nonumber \\
& + 0.20 \cdot (S_{graph} \cdot S_{rel}) \nonumber \\
& + 0.20 \cdot V_{agree} + 0.20 \cdot \Phi_{comp} \nonumber \\
& + 0.10 \cdot \Omega_{nov} - 0.15 \cdot R_{contra} \nonumber \\
& - \Pi_{penalty}\Big)\Big)
\label{eq:composite_confidence}
\end{align}
where the dynamic penalty term $\Pi_{penalty}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Pi_{penalty} = \; & 0.25 \cdot (1 - \mathbb{I}_{vision\_ok}) \\
& + 0.15 \cdot \sigma_{vision} + 0.10 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{evidence < 2}
\end{split}
\label{eq:penalty}
\end{equation}
In Eq.~(\ref{eq:penalty}), $\mathbb{I}_{vision\_ok}$ is an indicator function equal to $1$ when $\text{VisionStatus} = \textsc{Ok}$ and $0$ otherwise; $\sigma_{vision}$ represents visual epistemic uncertainty; and $\mathbb{I}_{evidence < 2}$ penalizes recommendations backed by fewer than two independent guideline passages.

\subsection{ACMR Dynamic Routing State Machine}
The orchestrator updates state across iterations $t \in [0, T_{max})$ according to the policy:
\begin{equation}
\resizebox{0.91\columnwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
\mathcal{A}(t) = 
\begin{cases}
\textsc{Abstain-Evidence}, & \text{if } \text{req\_img} \land \text{v\_st} \neq \textsc{Ok} \\
\textsc{Request-Input}, & \text{if } \text{v\_st} = \textsc{Low\_Quality} \\
\textsc{Escalate-Human}, & \text{if } \text{is\_critical} \\
\textsc{Safety-Alert}, & \text{if } R_{contra} \ge 0.50 \lor \text{Alert} \\
\textsc{Stop-Confident}, & \text{if } C_{total} \ge \tau_{high} \land \text{v\_st} = \textsc{Ok} \\
\textsc{Stop-Saturated}, & \text{if } \text{Overlap} \ge 0.85 \lor t \ge T_{max} \\
\textsc{Retrieve-More}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$}
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ACMR Dynamic Multimodal Execution Loop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Clinical case $x = (text, scan, labs)$, thresholds $\tau_{high}=0.80$, $\tau_{low}=0.50$, max iterations $T_{max}=2$
\ENSURE Structured Clinical Report $\mathcal{R}$
\STATE $v \leftarrow \textsc{IngestionQC}(scan)$
\IF{$v.status == \textsc{Low\_Quality}$}
    \RETURN $\textsc{Reasoner.reject}(v.quality)$
\ENDIF
\STATE $V_{res} \leftarrow \textsc{PluggableVisionEngine}(scan, v.metadata)$
\STATE $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow \textsc{Planner}(text, V_{res}.findings)$
\STATE $\mathcal{S}_0 \leftarrow \emptyset$, $t \leftarrow 0$
\WHILE{$t < T_{max}$}
    \STATE $E_t \leftarrow \textsc{Retriever}(\mathcal{P}.queries, V_{res})$
    \STATE $V_t \leftarrow \textsc{Verifier}(text, E_t, V_{res}, \mathcal{S}_t.previous\_chunks)$
    \STATE Compute $C_{total}^{(t)}$ via Eq.~(\ref{eq:composite_confidence})
    \STATE $\mathcal{A}_t \leftarrow \textsc{ACMRRouter}(C_{total}^{(t)}, V_t, V_{res}.status, \Delta C_t, t)$
    \IF{$\mathcal{A}_t \neq \textsc{Retrieve-More}$}
        \STATE \textbf{break}
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $\mathcal{P}.queries \leftarrow \textsc{Planner.adapt}(text, V_t.gap)$
    \STATE $t \leftarrow t + 1$
\ENDWHILE
\STATE $\mathcal{R} \leftarrow \textsc{Reasoner}(x, E_t, V_t, V_{res}, \mathcal{A}_t)$
\RETURN $\mathcal{R}$
\end{algorithmic}
\label{alg:acmr}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Benchmark Datasets and Cohort Construction}
We evaluate MedAgentX across multimodal imaging archives, an adversarial safety stress test set, and gold-standard clinical guidelines, detailed in Table~\ref{tab:datasets}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Radiological Imaging Cohort (121 Studies):} Sourced from the open-access VQA-RAD archive \cite{lau2018dataset} (CC0 license) and credentialed clinical research subsets \cite{johnson2019mimic}, spanning 45 Chest X-Rays, 65 Head/Chest/Abdomen CT scans, and 11 Brain/Spine MRI scans.

\subsection{Baseline Systems}
We compare MedAgentX against four established comparative baseline architectures (denoted B0 through B3, where B3 corresponds to the fixed multi-agent architecture B7 in repository benchmark logs):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{B0: Standalone LLM-Only:} Single-pass autoregressive prompting with no external retrieval or verification.
    \item \textbf{B1: Standard Vector RAG:} Dense retriever using BioLinkBERT \cite{yasunaga2022biolinkbert} against clinical guideline chunks.
    \item \textbf{B2: Medical RAG (PubMed):} Vector RAG augmented with biomedical literature abstracts \cite{sayers2021pubmed,jin2019pubmedqa} and keyword re-ranking.
    \item \textbf{B3: Fixed 4-Agent Pipeline:} Sequential multi-agent pipeline executing all agents across fixed passes with no early stopping (codebase baseline B7).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics \& Annotation Methodology}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic Accuracy (\%):} Measured on standardized clinical diagnostic queries against consensus medical board answers.
    \item \textbf{Expected Calibration Error (ECE $\downarrow$):} Grouped into $M=10$ confidence bins $B_m$ to quantify alignment between confidence and empirical accuracy \cite{guo2017calibration}:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{ECE} = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \frac{|B_m|}{N} \Big|\text{acc}(B_m) - \text{conf}(B_m)\Big|
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Safety Abstention Compliance (\%):} Percentage of the 16 adversarial stress cases where the system correctly commanded \textsc{Abstain-Insufficient-Evidence} or \textsc{Request-More-Input} rather than asserting a normal examination.
    \item \textbf{Hallucination Rate (\%):} Evaluated via dual-blind annotation by two senior biomedical researchers. Every recommendation across all 122 generated CDSS reports was cross-checked against retrieved guideline passages; claims lacking explicit guideline provenance were scored as hallucinations.
    \item \textbf{Token Consumption \& Latency:} Prompt tokens expended and runtime latency. Reported latency reflects the core ACMR orchestration loop (vector search, graph traversal, confidence scoring, and routing transitions).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Vision Timeout Fail-Closed Case Study}
In a representative emergency department scenario involving an acute Right Middle Lobe (RML) consolidation, high-resolution VLM CPU inference exceeded the 60s timeout threshold. In legacy systems, exception handling produced:
\begin{quote}
\textit{``Diagnostic Chest Examination without Acute Pathological Findings.''}
\end{quote}
Downstream agents retrieved normal guideline passages and emitted \textsc{Stop-Confident} ($C_{total}=0.905$), declaring a critically ill pneumonia patient completely healthy.

Under MedAgentX's fail-closed architecture:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The timeout event transitions $\text{VisionStatus} \leftarrow \textsc{Timeout}$ with $\text{findings} \leftarrow \emptyset$.
    \item The confidence penalty applies ($\Pi_{penalty} \ge 0.25$), suppressing $C_{total}$.
    \item Router Hard-Gate 1 triggers immediately: $\text{req\_img} \land \text{v\_status} \neq \textsc{Ok}$ prohibits \textsc{Stop-Confident} and commands \textsc{Abstain-Insufficient-Evidence}.
    \item The Clinical Reasoner generates a mandatory audit notice:
    \begin{quote}
    \textbf{NOTICE: Automated imaging perception timed out after 3 retry attempts. Diagnostic interpretation aborted. STAT human radiologist overread required.}
    \end{quote}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}
MedAgentX demonstrates that clinical reliability in AI cannot rely solely on larger foundation models. By enforcing structural separation between perceptual feature extraction and guideline reasoning, the system prevents perceptual hallucination from polluting clinical reasoning. Furthermore, deterministic confidence calculation ($C_{total}$) addresses the well-documented overconfidence of autoregressive models, providing clinicians with calibrated uncertainty.

The 80.7\% reduction in token consumption compared to fixed multi-agent pipelines (and 30.3\% vs.\ Vector RAG) highlights the practical utility of adaptive early stopping in high-throughput hospital settings.

\section{Limitations}
While MedAgentX advances safe multimodal decision support, several limitations warrant note:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Local Inference Latency:} Although the ACMR reasoning orchestration operates deterministically in 0.198s, local CPU execution of high-resolution vision models requires 15.2s--42.0s, necessitating dedicated GPU acceleration for high-throughput emergency departments.
    \item \textbf{Curated Benchmark Scale \& Class Balance:} The validation cohort contains a high proportion of negative studies, yielding wide sensitivity confidence intervals for rare pathologies. Prospective multi-center clinical trials with balanced positive cohorts are required for clinical adoption.
    \item \textbf{Knowledge Graph Coverage:} Rare pathologies or off-label pharmaceutical indications not captured in structured ontologies (UMLS, SNOMED) require manual expansion of the graph knowledge store.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Ethical Considerations \& Regulatory Alignment}
MedAgentX is engineered as an assistive clinical copilot and does not exercise autonomous diagnostic or prescriptive authority. In compliance with HIPAA Safe Harbor regulations (45 CFR \S 164.514(b) \cite{hipaa2002safeharbor}), all DICOM headers are stripped of direct patient identifiers prior to processing. Furthermore, following FDA Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) \cite{fda2017samd} and IEC 62304 \cite{iec62304} standards, the system incorporates mandatory clinician-in-the-loop audit gates for any critical pathology or safety abstention event.

\section{Conclusion}
MedAgentX addresses two persistent hazards in clinical AI: miscalibrated verbal confidence and dangerous fail-open perception fallbacks. Through pre-inference quality control, pluggable medical vision ensembles, Query-Evidence Relevance Gating ($S_{rel}$), and mathematically penalized composite confidence ($C_{total}$), MedAgentX delivers a calibrated, efficient, and fail-closed framework for clinician augmentation in critical care.
