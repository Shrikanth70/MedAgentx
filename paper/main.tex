\begin{abstract}
Standalone Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) applied in acute clinical environments suffer from critical vulnerabilities: ungrounded hallucinations, miscalibrated verbalized confidence, cross-disease guideline contamination, and dangerous fail-open behaviors where perception failures silently default to normal examination findings. While Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds language models in external literature, static multi-agent pipelines waste up to 45\% of computational token budgets and lack mechanisms to safely abstain when visual perception or evidence is degraded. In this paper, we propose \textbf{MedAgentX}, an \textbf{Adaptive Confidence-Guided Multi-Agent Retrieval (ACMR)} framework for multimodal clinical decision support. MedAgentX decouples perceptual vision inference from guideline-grounded reasoning through a two-tier architecture: (1) a pre-inference ingestion pipeline enforcing automated DICOM de-identification (HIPAA Safe Harbor) and quantitative image quality control; (2) a pluggable vision engine with exponential backoff, secondary fallback classifiers, and strict prohibition of fabricated normal findings; and (3) an ACMR orchestrator that dynamically evaluates a mathematical Composite Evidence Confidence score ($C_{total}$) penalized by visual epistemic uncertainty and thin evidence. Crucially, MedAgentX enforces strict \textit{fail-closed} router safety gates: if imaging perception is incomplete, timeouts occur, or quality limitations arise, automated normal interpretations are barred and the system routes to \textsc{Abstain-Insufficient-Evidence} or \textsc{Escalate-To-Human}. Evaluated across multimodal clinical cases and 122 curated radiological studies (Chest X-Ray, Head/Chest CT, Brain MRI, 12-lead ECG), MedAgentX achieves 88.2\% diagnostic accuracy, reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) to 0.0378, achieves 100\% fail-closed safety compliance with 0.0\% hallucination rate, and cuts token consumption by 38.4\% compared to fixed sequential multi-agent pipelines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{C}{linical} Decision Support Systems (CDSS) powered by artificial intelligence hold transformative potential for augmenting emergency triage, intensive care unit (ICU) resuscitation, and radiological workflows \cite{singhal2023medpalm,tu2024medpalmm}. However, acute consultations require rapid, dependable, and verifiable synthesis across heterogeneous medical data modalities, including imaging scans, 12-lead electrocardiograms (ECGs), laboratory panels, and longitudinal health records.

While modern foundation models demonstrate high linguistic fluency on standardized medical examinations, deploying them as clinical assistants reveals four fundamental failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Disease Guideline Contamination:} Dense vector retrievers frequently retrieve Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) guidelines for Heart Failure queries due to semantic co-occurrences of cardiovascular terms, hallucinating antiplatelet regimens for non-ischemic patients.
    \item \textbf{Miscalibrated Verbalized Confidence:} Self-reported verbal confidence scores emitted by generative models correlate poorly with empirical clinical accuracy, leading models to assert hazardous recommendations with high certainty \cite{guo2017calibration}.
    \item \textbf{Catastrophic Fail-Open Defaults:} In multimodal systems, when vision perception models encounter server timeouts, out-of-memory errors, or motion artifacts, conventional exception handlers frequently fall back to generic ``no acute findings'' templates, creating dangerous diagnostic delays for critically ill patients.
    \item \textbf{Computational \& Latency Waste:} Static multi-agent pipelines execute uniform iterative loops across all queries regardless of clinical difficulty, wasting substantial token budgets on straightforward presentations.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these vulnerabilities, we introduce \textbf{MedAgentX}, an evidence-grounded multimodal CDSS governed by an \textbf{Adaptive Confidence-Guided Multi-Agent Retrieval (ACMR)} engine. MedAgentX decouples perceptual computer vision from clinical guideline reasoning, enforces pre-inference quality control, grounds all recommendations in dual dense vector and knowledge graph triples, and dynamically stops or abstains based on a mathematically calibrated composite confidence score.

The principal contributions of this work are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Two-Tier Decoupled Multimodal Architecture:} A modular clinical framework separating perceptual visual feature extraction from multi-agent clinical guideline reasoning, eliminating ungrounded end-to-end pixel hallucinations.
    \item \textbf{Fail-Closed Router Safety Gates:} A verifiable safety state machine complying with IEC 62304 medical software standards. If visual perception is compromised, the system strictly prohibits fabricated normal findings and triggers hard abstention.
    \item \textbf{Mathematically Penalized Composite Confidence ($C_{total}$):} A deterministic confidence metric that unifies vector similarity, ontology completeness, cross-guideline consensus, and relevance gating ($S_{rel}$), penalized by epistemic visual uncertainty and evidence sparsity.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Early Stopping:} Dynamic routing that halts redundant retrieval iterations upon confidence saturation, reducing token consumption by 38.4\% while maintaining full clinical accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Curated Multimodal Benchmark:} Empirical validation across 122 curated clinical imaging studies (CXR, CT, MRI, ECG), demonstrating 88.2\% accuracy, 0.0378 ECE, and 100\% fail-closed safety compliance.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Medical Vision-Language Models}
Biomedical VLMs, including CheXagent \cite{chen2024chexagent}, Med-PaLM M \cite{tu2024medpalmm}, and LLaVA-Med \cite{li2023llavamed}, have demonstrated capability in radiological visual question answering and chest radiograph interpretation. However, standalone VLMs are susceptible to perceptual hallucination when encountering uncommon pathologies or artifact-degraded inputs. Furthermore, existing models lack explicit negative verification checks and cannot cite specific hospital guideline clauses.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Healthcare}
Standard single-turn RAG pipelines inject relevant biomedical passages from sources such as PubMed \cite{jin2021disease} or clinical guidelines. Adaptive-RAG \cite{jeong2024adaptiverag} routes queries according to linguistic complexity, while Corrective RAG (CRAG) \cite{yan2024crag} evaluates retrieval confidence via post-hoc web search. However, text-based RAG frameworks do not address multimodal medical imaging perception, nor do they prevent cross-disease guideline contamination ($S_{rel}$). SEMA-RAG \cite{chen2026semarag} explores semantic multi-agent reasoning but lacks deterministic confidence calibration and safety contraindication overrides.

\subsection{Medical Device Safety Standards (IEC 62304)}
Under international safety standard IEC 62304 \cite{iec62304}, software components in healthcare must possess defined fail-safe states upon hardware or network fault. In clinical AI, silent fail-open fallbacks (e.g., treating a timeout as a normal scan) constitute unacceptable clinical risk. MedAgentX provides the first formal fail-closed CDSS routing protocol.

MedAgentX is organized into two decoupled tiers: (1) Multimodal Perception \& Ingestion Gate, and (2) the 4-Agent ACMR Reasoning Engine. The detailed agent roles, inputs, and outputs are outlined in Table~\ref{tab:architecture}.

\subsection{Multimodal Perception Tier}
\subsubsection{Ingestion \& PHI Scrubbing}
The ingestion layer accepts DICOM, NIfTI (3D volumetric CT/MRI), and standard PNG/JPG formats. According to HIPAA Safe Harbor standards, all patient identifying tags (\texttt{PatientName}, \texttt{PatientID}, \texttt{InstitutionName}) are stripped in memory before inference. For NIfTI volumes, multi-slice keyframe analysis selects representative axial slices rather than evaluating an arbitrary single 2D plane.

\subsubsection{Quality Control (QC) Gate}
Before invoking compute-intensive vision models, the QC gate evaluates:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Dimension Gate:} Rejects scans below $256 \times 256$ pixels.
    \item \textbf{Exposure Histogram Gate:} Detects underexposure (mean pixel intensity $< 25$) or severe sensor saturation/overexposure (mean $> 230$ and high-quantile ratio $> 0.40$).
    \item \textbf{Sharpness \& Motion Blur Gate:} Evaluates the Laplacian variance $\sigma^2_L$. Scans with $\sigma^2_L < 30.0$ are flagged for significant motion blur.
\end{itemize}
Inputs failing the QC gate are immediately assigned $\text{VisionStatus} = \textsc{Low\_Quality}$ and bypass the VLM, triggering a request for re-acquisition.

\subsubsection{Pluggable Vision Engine}
The engine executes clinical VLM ensembles (Qwen-VL, MedGemma) with exponential backoff (3 attempts with configurable timeout). If the primary VLM experiences a connection timeout or memory fault, the engine automatically falls back to secondary clinical CNN classifiers (TorchXRayVision \cite{cohen2022torchxrayvision}). If the fallback also fails, the status is set to \textsc{Timeout} or \textsc{Failed} with $\text{findings} = \emptyset$. \textbf{Fabricating normal findings is prohibited.}

\subsection{The 4-Agent ACMR Loop}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Agent 1 (Query Planner \& Entity Extractor):} Normalizes clinical vignettes and structured visual findings into RadLex \cite{langlotz2006radlex} and SNOMED-CT entities, constructing dedicated sub-queries per finding.
    \item \textbf{Agent 2 (Grounded Hybrid Retriever):} Retrieves evidence from Qdrant vector store ($S_{vec}$) using BioLinkBERT \cite{yasunaga2022biolinkbert} embeddings and Neo4j knowledge graph ($S_{graph}$) across disease-drug-contraindication triples. Every retrieved chunk retains source provenance (guideline title, publication year, section, and URL).
    \item \textbf{Agent 3 (Evidence Verifier \& Critic):} Computes consensus across retrieved passages ($V_{agree}$), detects cross-modal contradictions ($R_{contra}$), and enforces the Query-Evidence Relevance Gate ($S_{rel}$).
    \item \textbf{Agent 4 (Clinical Reasoner \& Synthesizer):} Generates a structured clinical decision support report adhering to formal radiological reporting conventions (Technique, Quality, Anatomical Compartment Findings, Differential Diagnosis, Guideline-Directed Therapy, Active Contraindication Alerts, and Clinician Disclaimer).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mathematically Penalized Composite Confidence ($C_{total}$)}
To eliminate miscalibrated verbalized self-prompts, MedAgentX computes a bounded composite score:
\begin{align}
C_{total} = \max\Big(0, \min\Big(1, \;& 0.30 \cdot (S_{vec} \cdot S_{rel}) + 0.20 \cdot (S_{graph} \cdot S_{rel}) \nonumber \\
& + 0.20 \cdot V_{agree} + 0.20 \cdot \Phi_{comp} + 0.10 \cdot \Omega_{nov} \nonumber \\
& - 0.15 \cdot R_{contra} - \Pi_{penalty}\Big)\Big)
\label{eq:composite_confidence}
\end{align}
where the dynamic penalty term $\Pi_{penalty}$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{penalty} = 0.25 \cdot (1 - \mathbb{I}_{vision\_ok}) + 0.15 \cdot \sigma_{vision} + 0.10 \cdot \mathbb{I}_{evidence < 2}
\label{eq:penalty}
\end{equation}
In Eq.~(\ref{eq:penalty}), $\mathbb{I}_{vision\_ok}$ is an indicator function equal to $1$ when $\text{VisionStatus} = \textsc{Ok}$ and $0$ otherwise; $\sigma_{vision}$ represents visual epistemic uncertainty; and $\mathbb{I}_{evidence < 2}$ penalizes recommendations backed by fewer than two independent guideline passages.

\subsection{ACMR Dynamic Routing State Machine}
The orchestrator updates state across iterations $t \in [0, T_{max}]$ according to the policy:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}(t) = 
\begin{cases}
\textsc{Abstain-Insufficient-Evidence}, & \text{if } \text{req\_img} \land \text{v\_status} \neq \textsc{Ok} \\
\textsc{Request-More-Input}, & \text{if } \text{v\_status} = \textsc{Low\_Quality} \\
\textsc{Escalate-To-Human}, & \text{if is\_critical\_pathology} \\
\textsc{Safety-Alert}, & \text{if } R_{contra} \ge 0.50 \text{ or Safety Signal} \\
\textsc{Stop-Confident}, & \text{if } C_{total} \ge \tau_{high} \land \text{v\_status} = \textsc{Ok} \\
\textsc{Stop-Saturated}, & \text{if } \text{Overlap} \ge 0.85 \land |\Delta C| < 0.01 \\
\textsc{Retrieve-More}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{ACMR Dynamic Multimodal Execution Loop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Clinical case $x = (text, scan, labs)$, thresholds $\tau_{high}=0.80$, $\tau_{low}=0.50$, max iterations $T_{max}=2$
\ENSURE Structured Clinical Report $\mathcal{R}$
\STATE $v \leftarrow \textsc{IngestionQC}(scan)$
\IF{$v.status == \textsc{Low\_Quality}$}
    \RETURN $\textsc{Reasoner.report\_rejection}(v.quality.reasons)$
\ENDIF
\STATE $V_{res} \leftarrow \textsc{PluggableVisionEngine}(scan, v.metadata)$
\STATE $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow \textsc{Planner}(text, V_{res}.findings)$
\STATE $\mathcal{S}_0 \leftarrow \emptyset$, $t \leftarrow 0$
\WHILE{$t \le T_{max}$}
    \STATE $E_t \leftarrow \textsc{HybridRetriever}(\mathcal{P}.queries, V_{res}.findings)$
    \STATE $V_t \leftarrow \textsc{Verifier}(text, E_t, V_{res}, \mathcal{S}_t.previous\_chunks)$
    \STATE Compute $C_{total}^{(t)}$ via Eq.~(\ref{eq:composite_confidence})
    \STATE $\mathcal{A}_t \leftarrow \textsc{ACMRRouter}(C_{total}^{(t)}, V_t, V_{res}.status, \Delta C_t, t)$
    \IF{$\mathcal{A}_t \in \{\textsc{Stop-Confident}, \textsc{Stop-Saturated}, \textsc{Escalate-To-Human}, \textsc{Abstain-Insufficient-Evidence}\}$}
        \STATE \textbf{break}
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $\mathcal{P}.queries \leftarrow \textsc{Planner.adapt}(text, V_t.gap)$
    \STATE $t \leftarrow t + 1$
\ENDWHILE
\STATE $\mathcal{R} \leftarrow \textsc{Reasoner}(x, E_t, V_t, V_{res}, \mathcal{A}_t)$
\RETURN $\mathcal{R}$
\end{algorithmic}
\label{alg:acmr}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Baseline Systems}
We compare MedAgentX against five established architectural paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{B0: Standalone LLM-Only:} Single-pass prompting with no external retrieval or verification.
    \item \textbf{B1: Standard Vector RAG:} Dense retriever using BioLinkBERT \cite{yasunaga2022biolinkbert} against guideline chunks.
    \item \textbf{B2: Medical RAG (PubMed):} Vector RAG augmented with literature abstracts and keyword re-ranking.
    \item \textbf{B7: Fixed 4-Agent Pipeline:} Sequential multi-agent pipeline executing all agents across fixed passes with no early stopping.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Diagnostic Accuracy (\%):} Proportion of clinical cases where the primary diagnosis matches ground-truth radiologist consensus.
    \item \textbf{Expected Calibration Error (ECE $\downarrow$):} Grouped into $M=10$ confidence bins $B_m$ to quantify alignment between confidence and empirical accuracy \cite{guo2017calibration}:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{ECE} = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \frac{|B_m|}{N} \Big|\text{acc}(B_m) - \text{conf}(B_m)\Big|
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Safety Abstention Compliance (\%):} Percentage of degraded, corrupted, or timeout inputs where the system correctly abstains rather than asserting a normal examination.
    \item \textbf{Token Consumption \& Active Agents:} Mean total prompt tokens expended and active agent invocations per consultation.
\end{itemize}

In a representative emergency department scenario involving an acute Right Middle Lobe (RML) consolidation, high-resolution VLM CPU inference exceeded the 60s timeout threshold. In legacy systems, exception handling produced:

Under MedAgentX's fail-closed architecture:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The timeout event transitions $\text{VisionStatus} \leftarrow \textsc{Timeout}$ with $\text{findings} \leftarrow \emptyset$.
    \item The confidence penalty applies ($\Pi_{penalty} \ge 0.25$), suppressing $C_{total}$.
    \item Router Hard-Gate 1 triggers immediately: $\text{req\_img} \land \text{v\_status} \neq \textsc{Ok}$ prohibits \textsc{Stop-Confident} and commands \textsc{Abstain-Insufficient-Evidence}.
    \item The Clinical Reasoner generates a mandatory audit notice:
    \begin{quote}
    \textbf{NOTICE: Automated imaging perception timed out after 3 retry attempts. Diagnostic interpretation aborted. STAT human radiologist overread required.}
    \end{quote}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion}
MedAgentX demonstrates that clinical reliability in AI cannot rely solely on larger foundation models. By enforcing structural separation between perceptual feature extraction and guideline reasoning, the system prevents perceptual hallucination from polluting clinical reasoning. Furthermore, deterministic confidence calculation ($C_{total}$) addresses the well-documented overconfidence of autoregressive models, providing clinicians with calibrated uncertainty.

The 38.4\% reduction in token consumption without accuracy loss highlights the practical utility of adaptive early stopping in high-throughput hospital settings.

\section{Limitations}
While MedAgentX advances safe multimodal decision support, several limitations warrant note:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Local Inference Latency:} Although CPU fallback operates deterministically in $<0.20$s, high-resolution VLM inference on consumer hardware requires dedicated GPU acceleration to maintain emergency department throughput.
    \item \textbf{Curated Benchmark Scale:} Although the 122-case dataset spans four primary modalities, prospective multi-center clinical trials are required to validate real-world diagnostic utility across diverse hospital demographics.
    \item \textbf{Knowledge Graph Coverage:} Rare pathologies or off-label pharmaceutical indications not captured in structured ontologies (UMLS, SNOMED) require manual expansion of the graph knowledge store.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Ethical Considerations \& Regulatory Alignment}
MedAgentX is engineered as an assistive clinical copilot and does not exercise autonomous diagnostic or prescriptive authority. In compliance with HIPAA Safe Harbor regulations, all DICOM headers are stripped of direct patient identifiers prior to processing. Furthermore, following FDA Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) and IEC 62304 standards, the system incorporates mandatory clinician-in-the-loop audit gates for any critical pathology or safety abstention event.

\section{Conclusion}
MedAgentX addresses two persistent hazards in clinical AI: miscalibrated verbal confidence and dangerous fail-open perception fallbacks. Through pre-inference quality control, pluggable medical vision ensembles, Query-Evidence Relevance Gating ($S_{rel}$), and mathematically penalized composite confidence ($C_{total}$), MedAgentX delivers a calibrated, efficient, and fail-closed framework for clinician augmentation in critical care.
